What takes place between a subsurface target and the first barrel of saleable oil.

1. Establish the prospect

Geoscientists assemble maps, nearby well histories, seismic interpretations, logs and geological models. The objective is to identify a reservoir interval that may contain movable hydrocarbons. Each tool reduces some uncertainty; none can prove that a new location will produce commercially before drilling and testing.

2. Secure access, plans and permits

Before a rig moves, the project needs the necessary property interests, governmental authorizations, access roads, stable site preparation, an engineered well design and contractor planning. Weather, surface conditions and regulatory timelines can materially affect the schedule.

3. Drill, evaluate and case the well

The rig drills through rock sections while the crew manages circulating drilling fluids and well control. Geological samples, mud logs, wireline logs and other tests inform decisions. Casing and cement protect the wellbore and isolate targeted intervals as required by design and regulation.

4. Complete the well

A drilled well is not automatically a producing well. Completion may include preparing the selected interval, installing tubing and artificial lift, conducting tests and connecting the well to surface facilities. Operators assess technical and commercial results before continuing.

5. Commission, market and operate

Production requires appropriate separation, storage, metering, water handling, purchaser arrangements and regulatory authorizations. A sustained producing rate is established through performance data, not simply a short initial test. Ongoing maintenance and eventual plugging are part of the lifecycle.
